Rendering fidelity · 946 real-world office documents

Two renderers measured against LibreOffice

Every document in the corpus was rendered three ways — by headless LibreOffice, by XRay.Office (pure C#, document → PDF), and by the Rust/WASM viewer (Canvas in headless Chromium) — rasterised to the same pixel grid and compared page by page. LibreOffice is the gold standard; the question is how close the other two get, and where they part company with it.

The headline

Scored on the 639 word-processing and presentation documents, where all three engines produce printed pages and the comparison is like-for-like. Spreadsheets are held back to their own section: the WASM viewer has no print pagination, so its spreadsheet output is a screen grid rather than a page.

How a page was compared

Same document, same DPI, same pixel box — then several metrics rather than one, because no single number separates “a shape is missing” from “everything moved down three pixels.”

STEP 1

Reference

soffice --convert-to pdf on all 946 documents, then rasterised at 150 dpi. Metric-compatible substitute fonts (Carlito, Caladea, Liberation) installed first — a silent font substitution invalidates every number after it.

STEP 2

Candidates

XRay.Office writes its own PDF, rasterised identically. The viewer is driven through Playwright: load, wait for layout, render each page onto a canvas sized to the page's own point dimensions at 150 dpi, screenshot the canvas.

STEP 3

Align

Both sides land on the reference page's pixel box; anything off by rounding is resampled onto it. Pages the engine never produced are scored as zero rather than skipped. First five pages of each document.

STEP 4

Score

Structural similarity (SSIM) is the headline. Alongside it: mean absolute error, ink ratio, 32 px tile error, and a shifted-tile count that detects reflow. A page is called defective only when two independent metrics agree.

Per category

Fidelity is mean SSIM against the reference page, over the documents the engine actually rendered. Corpus fidelity is the same figure with every document the engine could not open counted as zero — the difference between the two columns is entirely format coverage.

The shape of the distribution

Means hide the tail. These are the same documents, counted into fidelity bands — what matters operationally is how much of the corpus sits above 0.90 and how long the bad tail is.

Format coverage

The single largest difference between the two engines is not fidelity, it is what they will open at all. XRay.Office reads the legacy binary formats; the viewer is OOXML-only and says so explicitly rather than failing obscurely.

A page qualifies only if a second metric corroborates the low SSIM — otherwise the list fills up with pages that are visually identical and merely resampled differently.

Spreadsheets and the viewer: a category difference, not a fidelity gap

The viewer's public API has renderViewport(sheet, {row, col, rows, cols}) and no pagination: it draws a scrollable grid with row and column headers, at screen scale, with gridlines. LibreOffice fits the same sheet to paper — scaled, headerless, broken across pages. Those are different artefacts, and no alignment makes the pixel comparison mean what it means elsewhere.
